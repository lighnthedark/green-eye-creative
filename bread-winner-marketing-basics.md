## The order that actually works

Most small businesses waste money by buying ads before the basics are in place. Here's the right order:

1. **Website** — already in motion, this is your home base. Every other channel points back to it.
2. **Google Business Profile** — free, and arguably more important than ads.
3. **Instagram/Facebook** — free, builds the relationship with your neighborhood.
4. **Word of mouth + local groups** — free, and for a bakery this is huge.
5. **Paid ads** — only once the free stuff is dialed in and you know what sells.

Skipping to step 5 first is how people burn money with nothing to show for it.

---

## Step 1: Google Business Profile (free — do this first)

When someone searches "sourdough bakery near me" or "bread Austin," this is what shows up — your hours, photos, reviews, a map pin, and a link to your site.

- It's free to set up at google.com/business
- Add photos of the bread, your setup, you at work
- Ask happy customers to leave a review — this is the single biggest lever for local search
- Keep your hours and pickup info accurate, especially when you sell out early

This alone can bring in more new customers than most paid ads for a local food business.

---

## Step 2: Instagram (and/or Facebook)

For a bakery, this is basically your storefront window. People buy bread with their eyes first.

- Post the weekly featured bake — same rhythm as the website's "This Week's Bake"
- Behind-the-scenes shots (shaping, scoring, the oven) perform well — people like seeing the process
- Use Instagram/Facebook Stories to post "3 loaves left today" — this creates urgency and mirrors the sold-out feature on the site
- Reply to every comment and DM — for a local business, that responsiveness is part of the product

You don't need to post daily. Consistent (2-3x/week) beats frequent-but-random.

---

## Step 3: Word of mouth and local groups

This is the most underrated channel for a neighborhood food business:

- Nextdoor and local Facebook groups (search "[your neighborhood] Austin" or "Austin foodies") — a genuine post about a small local bakery tends to do very well there
- Farmers markets or pop-ups, even occasional ones, are a great way to get face-to-face repeat customers who then follow you online
- A simple referral nudge works too — "Bring a friend, they get $2 off their first loaf" costs you almost nothing and spreads naturally

---

## Step 4: The pre-order / scarcity flywheel

You already have a built-in advantage: you sell out. That's not a limitation, it's marketing.

- "Only baking 20 loaves this Saturday — order by Thursday" creates real urgency (not fake hype, actually true)
- Every sold-out post is proof the bread is worth waiting for
- Consider building an email or text list for regulars so they get first dibs before you post publicly — this rewards loyalty and takes pressure off social media reach

---

## Step 5: Paid ads (later, and small)

Once you know your best-selling item and have a couple months of organic posting under your belt, paid ads can help — but start small:

- Meta ads (Instagram/Facebook) let you target a radius around your pickup location — this is the main one worth trying for a local bakery
- Start with $5-10/day, not a big monthly budget, and test one ad at a time
- The best-performing ad for local food businesses is usually just a great photo + "Order by Thursday for Saturday pickup" — simple beats clever
- Skip broad platforms like TV/radio or general Google Search ads until you're established — they're expensive and not targeted enough for a neighborhood bakery
